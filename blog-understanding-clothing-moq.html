Understanding Clothing MOQ: A Complete Guide for New Brands

📅 Aug 31, 2026 ⏱ 7 min read ✍️ By Laien Garments Team

If you are new to the fashion industry, you have probably encountered the term MOQ — Minimum Order Quantity — and wondered why it matters so much. For new clothing brands, MOQ can be the difference between launching your dream label and getting stuck before you even start.

In this guide, we will explain exactly what clothing MOQ means, why factories require it, how to negotiate lower minimums, and why 100 pieces per style has become the sweet spot for emerging brands.

What Is MOQ in Clothing Manufacturing?

MOQ (Minimum Order Quantity) is the smallest number of units a manufacturer is willing to produce for a single order. In apparel, MOQ is usually calculated in two ways:

  • Per style MOQ: The minimum number of units for one specific design (e.g., one dress design)
  • Per color MOQ: The minimum number of units for each colorway within that style

For example, a factory might have an MOQ of 300 pieces per style, with 100 pieces per color. If you want to produce a t-shirt in black, white, and navy, you would need to order at least 100 of each color — 300 total.

Why Do Factories Have MOQs?

Factories do not set MOQs to exclude small brands. There are real operational reasons behind them:

  • Fabric minimums: Fabric suppliers often require mills to purchase hundreds of meters at a time. A factory cannot buy 20 meters of fabric for one small order.
  • Production efficiency: Setting up cutting tables, sewing lines, and quality checks takes time. Small orders spread these fixed costs across fewer units, making each piece expensive to produce.
  • Profitability: At very low volumes, the labor and overhead costs can exceed the revenue from the order.
  • Machine setup: Changing thread colors, needle types, and machine settings between orders takes time that factories need to account for.

💡 Pro Tip

Factories with lower MOQs usually achieve this by using in-stock fabrics (available fabrics they already hold) or by grouping small orders from multiple clients to meet fabric mill minimums.

Typical MOQs in the Clothing Industry

MOQ requirements vary widely depending on the factory type, location, and product complexity:

  • Large-scale factories (China, Bangladesh, Vietnam): 500 – 3,000+ pieces per style
  • Mid-size manufacturers: 200 – 500 pieces per style
  • Low MOQ specialists: 50 – 200 pieces per style
  • On-demand / digital printers: 1 – 50 pieces (but limited to basic styles like t-shirts and hoodies)

At Laien Garments, we specialize in the low MOQ range, offering 100 pieces per color per style. This gives new brands enough flexibility to test multiple designs and colorways without tying up tens of thousands of dollars in inventory.

How to Negotiate a Lower MOQ

If a factory's MOQ is higher than your budget allows, here are proven strategies to negotiate:

1. Use In-Stock Fabrics

Ask the factory if they have fabric already in stock. Since they do not need to place a new fabric order, they are often willing to lower the garment MOQ.

2. Simplify Your Design

Complex designs with multiple fabrics, trims, and construction details drive up MOQ. A simpler design is faster and cheaper to produce at low volumes.

3. Consolidate Colors

Instead of offering a style in 6 colors, start with 2 or 3. This helps you meet the per-color MOQ more easily.

4. Pay a Higher Per-Unit Price

Some factories will accept lower MOQs if you agree to a slightly higher unit price to offset their setup costs.

5. Build a Relationship First

Factories are more flexible with long-term partners. Start with their standard MOQ, prove you are a reliable client, then negotiate lower minimums for future orders.

Why 100 Pieces Is the Sweet Spot for New Brands

After working with hundreds of emerging brands, we have found that 100 pieces per color strikes the perfect balance:

  • Low enough to test: You can produce 3 styles in 2 colors each (600 total) for under $5,000 – $8,000.
  • High enough to profit: At 100 units, per-unit production costs are reasonable, leaving healthy margins for retail pricing.
  • Manageable inventory: 100 pieces of one colorway is a quantity most new brands can sell through in 30-60 days.
  • Factory-friendly: 100 pieces allows factories to run efficient production lines without losing money.
"Starting with 100 pieces per color lets you test the market, gather real customer feedback, and iterate on your second collection — without risking your entire budget on one style."

MOQ vs. Your Business Model

Your ideal MOQ also depends on how you sell:

  • Pre-order / crowdfunding: You can commit to a factory only after collecting orders, making higher MOQs less risky.
  • Dropshipping: You typically need zero inventory, but you lose control over quality and branding.
  • Wholesale to retailers: Retailers often order 50-200 units per style, so your production MOQ needs to cover multiple wholesale accounts.
  • Direct-to-consumer (DTC): You control pricing and branding, but you carry inventory risk. Low MOQ is essential in the early stages.

Common MOQ Mistakes New Brands Make

  • Ordering too much, too soon: Many brands overestimate demand and end up with dead stock.
  • Ignoring the per-color MOQ: A factory might say "300 per style" but require "100 per color." If you want 6 colors, that is 600 units.
  • Choosing the cheapest factory: Ultra-low prices often mean hidden costs, poor quality, or no recourse if something goes wrong.
  • Not asking about sampling MOQ: Some factories have separate MOQs for samples vs. bulk. Clarify this upfront.
